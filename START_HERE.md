## What the evidence establishes

At fixed shared complex current dimension k=4,8,16, receiver-centered verified exchange improves 35 of 36 cases on smooth, near-contact, layered and asymmetric geometries. Over each object's nine common receiver checkpoints, the object-summed anchor top-one gain captures 98.1–99.6% of best local one-decision opportunity. This is not a pointwise guarantee. Restricted incoming-candidate coverage produces the largest losses. A saved Maxwell replacement increases information volume and effective dimension while worsening the actual step, showing why residual alignment must accompany curvature.

The 36 cases belong to four existing objects, with correlated states and budgets. No new exchange nonlinear reconstruction or noise study is run. Output-error bounds needed for deterministic acceptance and complete-neighborhood stopping remain unavailable. Conditional verification buys update fidelity; it does not beat unchanged receiver runtime.

## Reproducibility

All 17 remote attempts are closed, including two failed starts. Inclusive occupied time is 4889.515 s; peak sampled device memory is 1872 MiB. Raw records, source identities, portable algebra tests, plotting data, failures and costs accompany the release. Forty-two historical vendor modules remain external/hash-identified, so this is an evidence/reanalysis archive rather than a self-contained GPU replay bundle.
